How to start running this autumn

From your first 60 seconds of jogging to 30 minutes non-stop in 8 weeks — with the right shoes, the right layers for Polish weather, and everything you need to stay visible after dark.

Key takeaways

  • Start with run–walk intervals, not continuous running — it's how most beginners reach 30 minutes without injury.
  • Three sessions a week with a rest day in between is enough to see real progress in 8 weeks.
  • Shoes are the one thing worth investing in — cushioning matters more than looks or brand.
  • Dress as if it's 10°C warmer than the thermometer says: you warm up fast once you start moving.
  • From October, visibility is safety. A light and reflective details are essential for evening runs.

Before your first run

Running is one of the most accessible sports there is: you can start from your front door, at any time of day, with very little equipment. But a good start makes all the difference between a habit that lasts and one that fades after two weeks.

Set a simple, concrete goal. "Run 30 minutes without stopping by the end of November" is far more motivating than "get fit". A local 5 km parkrun or charity run is a great target once you get there.

Plan your sessions like appointments. Pick three days — for example Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday — and put them in your calendar.

ℹ️If you haven't exercised for a long time, have a heart or joint condition, or are over 40 and new to sport, check with your doctor before starting a training plan.

Your 8-week run–walk plan

Each session starts with a 5-minute brisk walk to warm up and ends with 5 minutes of easy walking and gentle stretching. Run at a conversational pace: if you can't speak in full sentences, slow down.

Choosing your first running shoes

You don't need a racing shoe to start. What you need is cushioning that protects your joints, a comfortable fit with a thumb's width of space in front of your toes, and good grip on wet pavements and park paths. Try shoes on in the afternoon, when your feet are slightly larger, and wear your running socks.

What to wear for autumn runs

Polish autumn can go from 18°C in early October to below zero by late November. The rule of thumb: dress as if it's about 10°C warmer than it really is, and choose technical fabrics that wick sweat — cotton stays wet and makes you cold.

    Running after dark: be seen, stay safe

    After the clocks go back on 25 October, the sun sets in Warsaw before 16:30 — and by late November it's dark around 15:30. For most people that means the majority of weekday runs happen in the dark.

    • Be visible from every side: a clip-on flashing light on your back and reflective details on your shoes and clothing.
    • See where you're going: on unlit paths, a chest or head light lets you spot kerbs, roots and puddles.
    • Choose familiar, well-lit routes and tell someone where you're going.

    6 mistakes beginners make (and how to avoid them)

    Starting too fast

    Most new runners go out at a pace they can't hold. Slow down until you can chat — it will feel almost too easy, and that's the point.

    Doing too much, too soon

    Your heart and lungs adapt faster than tendons and joints. Stick to the plan even when you feel great.

    Skipping rest days

    Your body gets stronger while it recovers. Keep at least one day between runs.

    Running in old trainers

    Worn-out or fashion sneakers don't absorb impact. It's the most common cause of sore knees and shins.

    Wearing cotton

    Cotton holds sweat, gets heavy and chills you in the wind. Technical fabrics dry in minutes.

    Forgetting to hydrate

    For runs under an hour, drinking before and after is usually enough — carry water on longer runs.

    Frequently asked questions

    How often should a beginner run?

    Three times a week is ideal: often enough to progress, with a rest day between sessions so your body can recover. Even two sessions a week will bring results.

    How long does it take to run 5 km?

    If you follow a run–walk plan three times a week, most beginners can run 30 minutes non-stop after about 8 weeks. At an easy pace, that's roughly 4–5 km — so a first 5 km is usually a few weeks further away.

    Is it OK to run in the cold?

    Yes. With the right layers you can run comfortably well below zero. Cover your hands and head, warm up indoors for a few minutes and keep your first kilometre easy.

    How do I know my running shoes are the right size?

    Leave about a thumb's width between your longest toe and the end of the shoe. Your heel shouldn't slip, and nothing should press on the sides of your foot.

    Should I run every day?

    Not as a beginner. Rest days are when your muscles, tendons and bones adapt. Replace a run with walking, cycling or swimming if you want to move every day.
