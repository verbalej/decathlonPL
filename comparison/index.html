The quick verdict

Choose the MH100 if you…

  • Hike mostly in valleys, forests and lowland trails
  • Want the lightest option: 490 g vs 575 g (size L)
  • Need an everyday rain jacket for the city too
  • Want to spend 100 zł less

Choose the MH500 if you…

  • Head to the Tatras, Karkonosze or Bieszczady in all weather
  • Walk for hours in heavy rain — 20,000 mm vs 15,000 mm
  • Sweat on long climbs and want 29 cm side vents
  • Carry a backpack often: the 3-layer build is more durable

For mountain hiking, the MH500 is worth the upgrade — especially at 299,99 zł until 2 October.

What the numbers actually mean

Waterproofing: 15,000 vs 20,000 mm

The Schmerber test measures how tall a column of water a fabric can hold before it leaks. The higher the number, the more pressure it withstands — from heavy, wind-driven rain or from backpack straps pressing wet fabric against your shoulders.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Quechua MH100 jacket really waterproof?

Yes. It has a 2.5-layer membrane rated to 15,000 mm on the Schmerber test, which is enough for rain on typical hiking trails. For long days of heavy mountain rain, the MH500's 20,000 mm is the safer choice.

What's the difference between the MH100 and MH500?

The MH500 has a higher waterproof rating (20,000 vs 15,000 mm), a more durable 3-layer membrane and 29 cm side ventilation zips. The MH100 is lighter (490 vs 575 g in size L) and 100 zł cheaper.

Are these jackets warm?

Neither is insulated — they're shells. Wear a fleece underneath when it's cold, or choose the insulated NH500 for winter walks.

How do I keep my jacket waterproof?

Wash it regularly with a technical detergent and follow the care label: dirt and body oils reduce breathability and the water-repellent finish.
